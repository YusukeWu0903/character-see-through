# Facial motion route

1. Inspect the approved face image and identify which features are baked into the base. A bald high-resolution portrait may supply excellent facial pixels while still being unsuitable as an animatable base. Prepare a source-faithful feature-free backing before adding detached eyes, brows, or mouths; inspect it alone for residual ink, halos, missing nostrils, or flat skin patches. Preserve the original portrait and static composite for comparison.
   For a small face mapped from a larger portrait, audit every skin sample used for inpainting: a nearby row can already cross the cheek, ear, hair or jaw outline. Inspect the feature-free backing alone at native pixels and enlarged, including the complete former lash/iris perimeter; an attractive closed-eye composite can still conceal residual original ink or a rectangular skin patch.
2. Register left/right eye white, iris, lash, brow, and closed lid to the same full-canvas coordinates. Keep iris clipping tied to its own eye white and use a shared, measured gaze vector. Test open, intermediate, and closed blink frames plus cardinal and diagonal gaze. If the source lacks a convincing closed lid, keep the motion a candidate rather than promoting a generated fallback.
   Compare zero gaze and zero blink to the **previous reviewed assembly with the new eye overlays bypassed**, not merely to another render of the same new eye rig. A self-comparison cannot detect a changed neutral face. In the moving states, inspect the eye white without iris, the iris sprite alone, and the final masked composite; a cropped iris may carry lower-lid skin even when its centre looks correct.
   When transplanting from a larger aligned closed-eye portrait, check the closed-lid alpha in isolation on contrasting grounds before judging the pretty full-face composite. Exclude nearby brows, ears and broad skin plates; preserve the source's soft eyelid drawing with a feathered artwork-shaped mask. Compare the reference character's actual blink pulse and layer blend, not only the control names. Any change to transition style needs open/half/closed moving-frame review and a versioned rollback.
3. Transplant mouth and expression pixels from user-supplied artwork where available. Do not redraw a source-specific shape by default. Trim stray edge pixels before measuring placement; compare each replacement against the feature-free base so two mouths or brows never appear at once. Retain lip color, nostrils, hair occlusion, and surrounding skin tone at native zoom.
4. Switch expressions by named combinations of brow, eye, lid, and mouth assets, not by flattening the entire face again. Validate neutral, smile, anger, speech mouth shapes, and each requested transition at the same registration. A crossfade can look like two superposed mouths; mask or staged interpolation must be visually checked at intermediate frames.
5. Compare with the approved static appearance, record source hash and any artistic deviations, and obtain separate user acceptance for blink/gaze, mouth, and expression candidates before default activation. Run the eye asset promotion skill when applicable.

## Curved-surface head-turn intake (before texture motion)

For a frontal head whose nose, mouth, brows, ears or cheek contour are still baked into one image, do not use a lateral pixel-shift field as a substitute for a yaw turn. If the reviewer sees neck/head twisting instead of facial perspective, mark that candidate rejected even if the neutral image and seam tests pass; retain the last accepted motion as the rollback.

First register facial landmarks against the exact source head and reference expressions, then test a **non-rendering geometry hypothesis**: show the inferred face surface, projected eye widths, nose, mouth and jaw at both signs of a small angle. Label the guide as hypothetical, default it off, and keep the underlying character pixels exactly identical to the prior candidate for every diagnostic slider value. Unlike an actual deformation-field guide, this projected wireframe is not evidence that the image itself turns. Record assumed depth, camera distance, angle, source hashes and visual mismatches in a versioned task QA. Do not promote a textured yaw until the face feature layers, occlusion order, missing side pixels and moving frames have separate source-backed review. Character-specific landmarks and geometry values belong in the task manifest, not this SKILL.

If the user requests a **pending visual texture trial** before full feature separation, warp only source-owned pixels in a separate reversible candidate and make the missing side-art limitation explicit. A projected dome can have a steep rim that folds a sampled texture even when still screenshots look plausible: verify mapping monotonicity/Jacobian at both extrema, fixed zero-weight boundaries, neutral identity, and unchanged pixels outside the declared owner. Clip its default-off *actual* deformation guide to the warped owner's alpha and let normal foreground layers occlude it. Test blink, gaze, previous head motion and return to neutral. This diagnostic exception does not authorize production promotion or invented hidden anatomy.

For an **up/down pitch request**, establish a screen-space landmark contract *before* choosing a formula: for each slider sign, record which end initiates motion, whether the opposite end is held or moves, and the expected direction and relative travel of hairline/forehead, eyes, nose, mouth and chin. If an initiating point does not settle the opposite anchor, ask rather than infer it. Do not replace a requested continuous curved push with a nose-bridge split or assume a uniform ellipsoid rotation gives the desired artistic compression. When the existing yaw surface is reused, derive the pitch guide and texture from the same surface evaluator and show actual intermediate and endpoint landmark travel, visible forehead height, owner boundaries and combined yaw/pitch states. Keep character-specific amplitudes in its candidate and preserve a rejected interpretation for comparison; a no-fold test proves geometry safety, not that the motion reads as looking up or down.

If pitch also needs **signed facial light**, write its above/below landmark regions and bright/dark signs separately from yaw cheek lighting. Register the boundary against the **current assembled face asset**, not inherited landmark numbers from a replaced head; inspect the actual painted nose bridge, tip and chin before tuning gain. Reuse an existing light switch and strength meaning when the user asks for control parity, but keep the new pitch response candidate-gated. At pitch zero, and at every pitch with light off, compare against the preceding rig pixel-for-pixel. Crossfade the region boundary, keep light inside the face's actual skin/alpha owner, and inspect brows, eye whites, nose ink, lips, jaw and neck in both signs and yaw/pitch corners. If the result is invisible, measure both magnitude and affected area at the default viewport before increasing gain; a changed-pixel count or unchanged alpha does not prove that the shade is perceptible or artistically acceptable.

For a provisional shape-from-shading test, do not assume a frontal PNG provides true normals or an unlit surface. Preserve the painted neutral exactly: infer a labelled hypothetical normal field from the already-reviewed geometry, keep a fixed provisional light, and apply only the yaw-dependent **difference** from the neutral diffuse term. Isolate a skin/face owner, cap brightness changes, and provide an independent on/off control so identical-pose pixels can be compared to the previous candidate. Verify neutral identity, off-path identity, unchanged alpha and out-of-owner pixels at both turns. Numerical change is not evidence that the face reads convex or that its new highlights suit the source art; require moving-frame artistic review and leave the public asset unchanged until accepted.

If the requested effect is **left/right cheek light exchange**, write the intended screen-side sign for each yaw extreme before implementation. A mathematically signed fixed side light may still read mainly as whole-face dark-versus-bright because the source has baked asymmetric lighting and pale highlights clip. Review isolated cheek deltas at both signs and intermediate angles, ensure the warm/shaded side does not wash out, and distinguish a physically motivated fixed-light test from an artist-directed bilateral balance. Preserve the failed candidate for comparison; do not silently change its parameters or treat opposite-sign pixel changes alone as artistic success.

When a reviewer further separates **upper-face lift from lower-face shadow**, clarify whether lower-face darkening remains. Keep the chosen positive and negative branches independent, anchor any vertical boundary to source landmarks rather than a universal pixel row, taper the positive lift smoothly so no horizontal band appears, and test that lower-face bright pixels are absent while the approved shadow branch is byte-identical. Record the character-specific falloff and gain only in its task candidate. This is still a visual hypothesis requiring moving-frame approval.
