# Body motion route

Use the existing Eris prototype in `docs/rig-roadmap.md` as architectural evidence, not a coordinate or amplitude template for another character. The current `viewer/deform-preview.mjs` fetches `eris-deform.json` and the canonical task PNG names rather than a reviewed assembly manifest; opening it with a new character task does **not** load that character's approved replacement head or seam assets. Adapt or build a task-manifest-aware candidate viewer before drawing conclusions from its motion.

1. Audit the approved assembly for source layers, draw slots, motion parents, occluded underfill, and seams. Calibrate head/neck, shoulder, chest, waist, hip, knee, ankle, and hair-root landmarks on this character. Record uncertainty where a landmark is hidden. Split joined limbs into left/right assets only if the source provides enough visible and hidden pixels to maintain coverage; otherwise constrain motion and label the limit.
2. Establish neutral identity first: loading the rig at zero motion must reproduce the accepted assembly at native resolution and draw order. Use full parent inheritance for attached parts, then add bounded local offsets. A repair patch must follow its owning surface; do not make it a fixed screen-space cover.
3. Build body sway, head movement, arm/leg behavior, and hair follow-through in separate candidate stages. Inspect head-to-neck, each shoulder, garment straps, armpits, waist, crotch, knees, feet, and hairline at neutral, both parameter extremes, and mid-transition. Test independent parent motion; a coupled motion pass does not certify an independent arm or head.
   For a small rigid head roll plus face-surface yaw, audit whether the neck is still parented to the torso and whether front/back hair already inherit head roll. If the visible neck must follow, deform only its owning layer: match a fraction of roll at the head junction, let yaw curve over a shallow cylinder, and smoothly reduce both to nearly zero at the clavicle. Preserve neutral pixels and verify there is no gap at jaw, ear, or shoulder in both directions. Do not move the whole torso to fake this coupling.
   If separate hair layers exist, keep their roots attached to the head; apply any extra yaw follow-through as a bounded, nonlinear length field rather than translating whole PNGs. Front and back may move in opposing directions when the art calls for it, but this is a character-specific choice, not a universal sign rule. Reuse the existing motion spring when possible and test rapid reversal for a small overshoot followed by settling, as well as pause, reset, zero yaw, both yaw extremes, and combined roll/yaw. State which head control drives the extra inertia; do not silently change the other control's established behavior. Show default-off influence guides computed from the rendered fields, and inspect scalp, ears, neckline, and long-hair occlusion in the live preview before acceptance.
   When a hair layer also has idle sway, avoid a second independent whole-layer motion that competes with its head-follow field. During active head control changes, let the turn own the hair target; after a short settled interval, blend idle back around the **current** head pose, including nonzero held yaw. Switching idle off should settle at that same pose, never jump to the zero-yaw location. Use a smooth bounded idle signal rather than per-frame random jitter. Verify turn→hold→idle, reverse, pause, idle-off, and neutral with both rendered pixels and motion-state traces.
4. Add chest/bust motion only after torso and garment seams pass. Separate underlying body response from clothing response, keep straps attached and occlusion stable, and calibrate character-specific amplitudes. A visible chest control is not evidence of a safe default; compare outlines and skin/clothing junctions through the full oscillation and after pause/reset. Do not copy Eris's numeric setting as a Miffy preset without review.
   When source art combines breast, skin and garment in one flattened surface, a bounded shared-composite warp is an honest candidate, not independent breast physics. Verify the neutral image is identical, pixels outside the declared field are unchanged, and the motion is perceptible at the intended viewport before inviting artistic acceptance.
   Before warping a full composite, check whether the affected skin and garment already share one source layer: a full-composite field may also drag foreground hair or arms through the chest. If stronger motion exposes rectangular tile boundaries or stair-stepped necklines, reject that candidate at its maximum setting; do not lower amplitude until it becomes invisible. Try a smoothly sampled field owned by the relevant source layer, preserve premultiplied-alpha edge quality, and compare minimum, maximum, and intermediate frames on the full composite. Keep the rejected candidate as evidence.
   When the intended effect includes both idle vertical response and pointer-driven lateral response, model and test them as separate bounded components before combining them. Reuse the character's existing idle/follow toggle semantics and eased pointer signal; the strength control may scale both, but idle-only must not create lateral drift and follow-only must not invent a vertical bounce. Verify rendered left/right direction, return after pointer leave or follow-off, pause/reset, and simultaneous extrema at the default view and maximum strength. A two-axis field must still preserve the source layer's outside pixels and skin/garment junctions; do not infer lateral motion from a nonzero vertical spring or from whole-body pointer sway.
   Review the chest field using the shared [visual influence review](deformation-field-review.md) gate: derive a default-off diagnostic overlay from the actual deformation weight function, not a separately guessed rectangle. Draw it in the owning layer's order and through the same parent/stance transform so occlusion stays truthful; label it as an influence guide when the implementation is a sampled pixel field rather than a vertex mesh. Test that enabling it changes only the declared region and disabling it restores the original canvas exactly. Never bake the guide into a source PNG or production export.
   When shortening one side of a field while increasing displacement, preserve a smooth zero-weight taper at the new boundary and verify the two-axis mapping remains non-folding at opposite maximum offsets. Compare the actual guide contour, not just the configured radius, and inspect the garment seam at both extremes; a mathematically valid mapping alone is not artistic acceptance.
5. Validate stable playback at representative frame rates and a deterministic reset to neutral. Keep stress-test ranges visibly non-production; do not reduce native texture resolution or quality to make the preview responsive. User acceptance of the actual moving preview is a separate gate before default activation.

When reusing a rig evaluator, declare whether its pivots and translation amplitudes are in normalized coordinates or native canvas pixels. Do not carry a normalized breathing offset into a pixel-space rig and call it breathing; leave that control disabled until its displacement and garment coverage are character-calibrated. Pin the motion candidate to the exact assembly manifest hash so a later seam edit cannot silently change the neutral reference, and use an in-browser native-canvas pixel comparison before reviewing motion.

A changed canvas hash only proves that animation is numerically nonzero. Check perceptual displacement at the actual default viewport and zoom: record a silhouette landmark's screen-pixel travel across an automatic cycle, watch the live preview, and reject a default that appears motionless. Keep rejected small-amplitude settings as a versioned candidate rather than silently revising a reviewed URL. If the reference viewer has controls the new character cannot yet support, show them as disabled with the missing-asset reason; expose all safe parity controls (layer visibility, view/zoom, pause, idle, pointer-follow, neutral/defaults, guides, and task-scoped calibration) so reviewers can inspect the same risks.

Classify the character's ground contacts before increasing body sway. A hip-centered whole-character rotation is not a planted standing motion: both feet orbit and tilt. Compare the reference rig's actual support pivot and shared deformation field, not just its slider names. When feet cannot have independent IK, use a shared, foot-grounded deformation candidate with the sole row fixed and common displacement for all overlapping layers; document that this is still not IK. Test each visible sole at both extrema and throughout the cycle for horizontal travel and unchanged ground height, as well as the upper-body travel required to make motion perceptible. A candidate that fixes seams but slides feet is rejected.

For a narrow, nearly straight standing pose, a single linear ground-to-head shear can keep the feet planted yet still look like a rigid tumbler. Before making it stronger, use a real standing reference and separate the supported hip shift, torso compensation, and head stabilization into small, staggered controls. Keep the exact amplitudes character-specific. If source legs are joined, a shared low-amplitude field may be reviewed as a visual cue, but do not claim knee/ankle articulation or physiological simulation. To avoid re-opening layer seams, apply a common deformation to the composited image or use a shared mesh field across overlapping layers; inspect resampling/banding artifacts at native zoom. Validate sole contacts, hip and head travel, pause/reset, and intermediate frames in the live viewer, then require artistic review before promotion. Useful primary references: [standing multi-joint coordination](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/jn.01142.2006), [Spine IK contact control](https://us.esotericsoftware.com/spine-ik-constraints), and [Live2D secondary-motion setup](https://docs.live2d.com/en/cubism-editor-manual/physical-operation-setting/).

When offering the same idle and pointer-follow toggles across characters, match their interaction semantics, not their numeric gains: pointer is additive to idle, uses a target relative to the character/view, eases by elapsed time rather than redrawing on every pointer event, freezes on pause, and returns smoothly after pointer leave. Calibrate follow amplitude to each stance so it does not overwhelm the idle pose. Test idle-only, follow-only, both, pause, toggle-off, and rapid pointer reversals in the live viewer; matching labels alone is insufficient.

Also verify the **rendered screen-space direction** with pointer left and right. A negative rotation control may move an above-pivot character toward screen-right, while a negative horizontal displacement moves it toward screen-left. Do not reuse the sign of another rig's follow gain without measuring the head/hip pixels on both sides; ensure the feet remain planted during that test.
