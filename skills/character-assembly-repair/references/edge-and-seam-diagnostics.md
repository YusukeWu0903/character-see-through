# Edge and seam diagnostics for an assembly candidate

Use this when an apparent line, halo, plate, or gap is visible at a join. Treat every character's source and registration as new evidence; do not reuse another character's pixel coordinates or repair mask.

1. Capture the same canvas crop in the unmodified assembly and candidate, at native scale and at a readable enlargement. Solo the suspected layer, the layer underneath, and their pair. Repeat over light, dark, and checkerboard backgrounds. A gray rectangle can be nearly invisible on white; transparent PNG RGB is not visible art unless alpha is nonzero.
2. Classify provenance before editing: painted source line; extraction edge RGB/alpha; two layers overlapping in the wrong order; missing underfill; or motion-parent tear. In particular, a dark shoulder line may exist in neither source layer alone but appear when a partially transparent, dark torso edge overlays an arm. Turning off the arm may hide the symptom without proving the arm is defective.
3. Prefer the smallest reversible correction to the owning asset: remove only proven exterior alpha or dark overlap alpha when intact source skin is already behind it; retain RGB and verify alpha never increases. If skin is genuinely missing, register a narrow source-pixel transplant and assign it to the moving surface it belongs to. A broad skin-colour plate is a last resort and must not cover garments, straps, hair, or an independently moving limb.
   If alpha and anatomy are intact but a few outline RGB pixels were washed into skin, compare those exact coordinates against the registered higher-resolution source. Restore only source-painted contour colour within a measured task-local band, leave alpha unchanged, and reject any attempt that thickens the line, introduces dark dots, or uses an invented stroke. Record the source and repaired pixel values, then inspect the line against both hair and light backgrounds.
4. Keep a named non-production candidate and rejected comparisons. Measure full-canvas size, changed-pixel bounds, RGB changes, alpha increases/decreases, and presence of opaque underfill at every removed pixel. A small bounding box alone does not prove visual correctness: look for pale spots, dotted contours, doubled outlines, and newly exposed background.
5. Use parent-specific movement probes: neutral, head up/down, torso shift, hair independent from head, and arm independent from torso, at modest and stress offsets appropriate to that character. A joined arm layer can move both arms together for diagnosis but cannot establish independent-arm rig quality. Record each pass or failure explicitly; do not mark seam or rig accepted when only the neutral frame passes.
